Home in the evening. Boil water. Make tea.

I have practised this small thing for a long time and still have not got it right — but I like that it **stays unmastered**.

## The hard part is waiting

Green tea is delicate; water too hot and it turns bitter. So you wait: for the water to fall from boiling to around eighty degrees, for the leaves to open slowly in the cup, for the colour to shift from pale yellow to a warm amber.

While waiting there is nothing to do but watch.

> The skill modern life most lacks is probably this: **letting something happen, and being at peace while it does**.

The phone is always buzzing in a pocket. Those three minutes waiting for the water are three minutes of blank I give back to myself.

## White space, in a cup

You do not fill the cup. Seven-tenths — three-tenths left for the steam, and three-tenths left so the hand holding it does not get burned.

Life is much the same:

- A schedule packed too tight goes deep into nothing
- Words spoken too absolutely leave no room to turn
- A heart filled to the brim has no place for anything new to land

**White space is not emptiness. It is a seat kept for the unexpected.**

## What one cup taught me

When the tea goes cold, you don't have to force it down. And today's waiting need not be saved up as tomorrow's debt.

Just this cup, this moment. The mountain outside the window, the water in the cup, time moving slowly.

*May you also find one thing worth doing slowly.*
