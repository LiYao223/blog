There is a sentence I keep returning to: **there is no truth, only perspective.**

Don't rush to object — it is not an invitation to slide into "anything goes" relativism. Quite the opposite. It is a clearer kind of honesty.

## We mistake perspective for truth

When two people argue themselves red in the face, it is rarely because one of them holds more facts. It is because **they are standing in different places**:

- The person at the foot of the mountain sees a rock blocking the path
- The person on the summit sees a ridge connecting into a line

Both may be telling "the truth." They are simply **describing the view from their own side**.

> Argument is not the danger. The danger is forgetting this: what the other person sees, I cannot see from where I stand.

## Perspective ≠ relativism

Admitting "I only have my perspective" is not the same as saying "all perspectives are equal."

Perspective carries **responsibility**:

1. Say clearly where you stand — instead of pretending to stand on the high ground of truth
2. Be willing to walk to the other side and see what the same rock looks like from there
3. Care more about **seeing the thing clearly** than about **winning the argument**

So the real teaching of "only perspective" is **humility**, not licence.

## What it changed in me

I used to read a dissenting opinion and immediately look for the flaw, preparing a counterattack. Now I pause and ask myself one question:

*From where he is standing, what did he see that I missed?*

Often the answer is: **quite a lot**.

This does not weaken my judgement. It gives the judgement roots. Only someone who knows they have a perspective has the right to talk about being clear-eyed.
