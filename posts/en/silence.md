Some books leave you quiet when you close them — and somehow full.

## Silence is a tone

We tend to assume a "good book" should be dense with quotable lines and sharp opinions. But there is another kind that does the opposite: it does not **rush to convince you**. It lays out a scene, places the characters in it, and then falls silent.

> The highest form of narration is when the author steps back and lets the characters speak for themselves.

This silence is not an absence. It is a form of **respect**: it trusts that the reader is able to see for themselves. We, who have been fed conclusions all our lives, find it uncomfortable at first — and then find it a relief.

## A passage I read recently

The book describes a city before a typhoon. Everyone carries private worries, and everyone quietly goes about the task at hand. No one steps forward to say "this means that." And it is precisely that **refusal to explain** that makes the gathering tension land on the reader, one beat at a time.

I copied it into my notebook:

- Real tension often has no soundtrack
- Real affection usually explains nothing
- Real structure hides in the seams of silence

## The courage to leave things unsaid

After finishing a piece, I often feel the urge to add one more "in conclusion." Lately I have been learning to resist it.

Some things are best said to seven-tenths, and the last three-tenths left to the reader and to yourself. Those three-tenths are where the writing comes alive.

*Next time a book makes you go quiet, don't scroll away too quickly — that may be exactly when it is speaking to you.*
